# Bahia Spatial Turnover Diagnostic

**Status: descriptive model-assumption diagnostic only. No Stan model was fit or modified. q was not estimated. Serology was not used to force susceptibility.**

## 0. Purpose and scope

The frozen homogeneous state-level Bahia renewal model (one `S_Bahia(t)`,
one ascertainment parameter, seasonal `R0(t)`) strongly prefers **high q
(median ≈0.36) and minimal statewide susceptible depletion** when serology
is removed (global-q identification ablation, `J_sero=0`). This is
mechanically forced by the demographic replenishment constraint: repeated,
similarly-sized statewide epidemics are only compatible with a homogeneous,
weakly-depleting susceptible pool, because lifelong immunity plus modest
annual births cannot refill a heavily-depleted statewide pool between
consecutive epidemics.

This diagnostic tests an alternative, purely descriptive explanation:
**successive Bahia-wide epidemics may be generated by different,
asynchronous municipal-level epidemics rather than the same population
being lightly re-exposed every cycle.** If true, state-level "high q / low
depletion" would not necessarily mean statewide ascertainment is truly
high -- it could instead reflect aggregation of spatially heterogeneous,
locally-depleting local epidemics that only look like mild depletion once
summed to the state level.

No Stan model, prior, or likelihood was touched in this task.

## 3-4. Municipality-level wave burden and successive-wave turnover

Per-municipality, per-wave incidence (per 100k), share of state cases, and
cumulative-prior incidence were computed for all 414 municipalities x 9
waves (`bahia_municipality_wave_burden.csv`).

**Adjacent-wave turnover metrics** (`bahia_wave_turnover_metrics.csv`):

| Metric | Median (adjacent waves) | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Spearman incidence correlation | 0.333 | 0.222 - 0.386 |
| Top-10% municipality Jaccard | 0.159 | 0.050 - 0.217 |
| Top-20% municipality Jaccard | 0.230 | 0.153 - 0.297 |
| 80%-case-contributor Jaccard | 0.109 | 0.000 - 0.179 |
| Share of next wave's cases from municipalities that were **zero-case** in the prior wave | 0.132 | 0.010 - 0.229 |
| Share of next wave's cases from municipalities in the **bottom 50%** of prior-wave incidence | 0.137 | 0.010 - 0.229 |

No adjacent-wave pair reaches a Spearman correlation above 0.39, and the
top-decile overlap is consistently low (roughly 1 in 5-10 top-incidence
municipalities carries over to the next wave). Between 1% and 23% of each
wave's total cases (median 13%) come from municipalities that reported
**zero** cases during the immediately preceding wave.

## 5. Prior burden versus future-wave incidence (descriptive only)

Contrary to a simple "hit hard once -> depleted -> quiet next time" story,
the pooled (all-wave) Spearman correlation between a municipality's
cumulative incidence **before** a wave and its incidence **during** that
wave is **positive**: rho = 0.352 (per-wave range 0.16-0.39, see
`bahia_prior_burden_per_wave_spearman.csv`). The highest prior-incidence
quintile of municipalities consistently has a *higher*, not lower,
population-weighted mean incidence in the next wave (e.g. wave 6: Q5
pop-weighted mean = 127/100k vs Q1 = 1.8/100k; wave 10: Q5 = 316/100k vs Q1
= 26/100k -- see `bahia_prior_burden_future_wave.csv` and
`bahia_prior_burden_vs_future_incidence.png` /
`bahia_prior_burden_quintile_summary.png`).

**Interpretation (descriptive, not causal):** this positive association is
most consistent with persistent structural risk factors (urbanization,
*Aedes* suitability, reporting infrastructure, population density) rather
than progressive, cumulative susceptible depletion suppressing future
incidence in previously-hit places. Reporting heterogeneity, climate, and
importation are all plausible confounders of this association and cannot
be disentangled with these data alone -- this is a model-criticism
observation, not evidence for or against true local susceptible depletion.

## 6. Spatial maps

`bahia_wave_incidence_maps.png` (same colour scale, capped at the 98th
percentile, across all 9 waves) shows epidemic activity concentrated in
visibly **different broad sub-regions of Bahia in different waves** --
e.g. a northern cluster in wave 02, a small southern coastal cluster in
waves 03-05, broad northern spread in waves 06-07, western/southwestern
hotspots in wave 08, and northwestern/southern activity in waves 09-10.

`bahia_wave_max_incidence_summary_map.png` (which wave produced each
municipality's peak incidence) shows clear **regional-scale clustering by
wave** -- e.g. the far north-west and north are dominated by wave 07/08,
while much of the south and south-east peaks in wave 09/10 -- rather than
either (a) a single wave dominating everywhere, or (b) a fully scrambled,
municipality-by-municipality random pattern. Of the 414 municipalities,
the wave producing their maximum incidence is spread across waves 02
(n=49), 03 (12), 04 (2), 05 (17), 06 (87), 07 (38), 08 (69), 09 (63), and
10 (77) -- no single wave accounts for a majority.

## 7. Full pairwise turnover matrix

`bahia_wave_pairwise_correlation_heatmap.png` (all 9x9 wave pairs, not
just adjacent) shows **every off-diagonal cell between 0.03 and 0.39** --
i.e. the low-to-moderate correlation found for adjacent waves holds

`bahia_top_municipality_overlap_heatmap.png` shows the same pattern for
top-10%/20% and 80%-contributor Jaccard overlap.

## 8. Recurrent-epidemic interpretation

Evidence assembled:

- **Against STRONG SPATIAL PERSISTENCE:** every pairwise Spearman
  correlation across all 9 major waves is <0.4; top-decile municipality
  overlap between waves is consistently low (~5-30%); 80%-contributor
  overlap is even lower (median 0.11); a substantial (median 13%, up to
  23%) share of each wave's burden comes from municipalities with zero
  cases in the immediately preceding wave; and the wave-of-maximum-
  incidence map shows clearly different sub-regions dominating different
  waves.
- **Against STRONG SPATIAL TURNOVER (pure/random reshuffling):** the
  correlations are not near zero (median ~0.33 for adjacent waves, and
  positive for every one of the 36 possible wave pairs); cumulative prior
  incidence is positively (not negatively or randomly) associated with
  subsequent-wave incidence; and the wave-of-maximum-incidence map shows
  clear **regional-scale spatial clustering** (contiguous sub-regions
  sharing a dominant wave), not a spatially scrambled, municipality-level
  random pattern.

**Classification: MIXED**

Some municipalities/sub-regions carry a persistently elevated relative
risk across waves (a moderate, positive, structural signal), while the
specific set of municipalities that dominates any given statewide wave
changes substantially and systematically from wave to wave, with
different broad geographic sub-regions taking turns generating the major
share of statewide cases. Both seasonal forcing acting on a
quasi-homogeneous pool AND geographically heterogeneous, at least
partially asynchronous local epidemics plausibly contribute to the
observed recurrent statewide pattern.

## 9. Connection to the current renewal model (conceptual only -- not refit)

| | Current state model | Observed municipality pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Susceptible pool | One `S_Bahia(t)`, homogeneous mixing | 414 municipalities with correlations of 0.03-0.39 between any two major waves -- far from homogeneous |
| Ascertainment | One state-level `q` | Cannot be tested here; unaffected by this diagnostic |
| Transmission timing | One seasonal `R0(t)` shared statewide | Different sub-regions peak in different waves -- partially asynchronous, not a single shared statewide clock |

The observed turnover **weakens, but does not eliminate, the plausibility
of the homogeneous-mixing assumption.** Because the turnover is
substantial (low pairwise correlations, low top-contributor overlap,
regional-scale wave dominance shifting over time) but not total (moderate
positive correlations persist, and some municipalities are consistently
above/below average), the state-level model's "high q / minimal
depletion" inference from the case-only ablation should be treated as
**one candidate explanation among at least two** for the recurring
statewide pattern:

1. True statewide ascertainment is genuinely high and depletion is
   genuinely minimal (the state model's own preferred reading absent
   serology), OR
2. Statewide recurrence partly reflects **aggregation of asynchronous,
   more strongly locally-depleting sub-epidemics**, which a homogeneous
   state-level model cannot distinguish from (1) because both produce a
   similar-looking, mildly-damped statewide time series.

This diagnostic cannot adjudicate between (1) and (2) -- it only
establishes that (2) is epidemiologically plausible given the observed
spatial turnover, and therefore that the state model's high-q/low-
depletion result should **not** be read as direct proof of high statewide
ascertainment.

## 10. Relation to the existing global-q + multisite-serology + eta_geo model

Given the MIXED classification (meaningful spatial turnover confirmed,
though not absolute), **`eta_geo` remains scientifically defensible** as
an observation-level allowance for local serosurvey sites differing from
Bahia's state-level infection-derived immunity -- this is exactly the kind
of geographic heterogeneity this diagnostic finds evidence for. The large
`eta_geo` values estimated for U03/U04/U21 (median 1.9-2.9 on the log-odds
scale) are therefore plausible rather than merely a sign of model misfit.

However, this analysis explicitly does **not** license interpreting the
`global q` posterior or the state-level `S_Bahia(t)` trajectory from that
model as uniquely identified. `eta_geo` is a purely observation-level
(likelihood-side) correction -- it does not model spatial transmission,
local depletion, or asynchronous timing, and it cannot, by construction,
feed information about *where* or *when* local epidemics occurred back
into the shared, homogeneous `S_Bahia(t)`/`q` recursion. The spatial
turnover documented here is a structural feature of the transmission
process that the current serology extension was never designed to
capture; it is a reason for **additional caution**, not resolution, when
interpreting `q` or statewide susceptibility from that model.

## Final classification

**MIXED** -- substantial spatial turnover of the specific municipalities
driving each statewide wave, combined with a persistent, moderate,
structural tendency for certain sub-regions to remain relatively
higher-risk across waves. This supports treating the homogeneous
state-level model's high-q / low-depletion reading with caution rather
than as confirmed evidence of true high statewide ascertainment, without
positively establishing the reverse.
